# Appendix B | The Strategic Executive Non-Intervention™ Assessment

The following is an invented workshop exercise. I have designed it to be calculated by a person in a position of their choosing. It is a check on decision habits and carries no diagnostic, professional or employment consequences.

## Step 1 | Use six matched questions

Choose a recent optional initiative. Respond to each item with **0** for no evidence, **1** for partly evidenced and **2** for clear evidence. Mark an item inapplicable if the question does not fit the case. Explain why in one sentence. I recommend choosing a case with at least four applicable items.

| Item | Question | Evidence worth looking for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Could I say why the work ought to happen before it appeared on a calendar? | A short statement of the desired change or real obligation |
| 2 | Did I know what the team could finish on its own authority? | A description of the work and the limits of delegation |
| 3 | Did I give engineering or operations credit for the evidence they produced? | A handoff or record with the work attributed accurately |
| 4 | If I deferred, did the team know it could stop? | A clear owner decision with a consequence |
| 5 | Was the next trigger small enough for one person to recognise? | A named event, authority or new fact |
| 6 | Could I physically leave the room while the disposition continued to make sense? | A record people could use without a guided reading |

Before assigning a figure, ask one person who was present what they understood to be required of them. If they give a less flattering answer than you expected, preserve their answer. There is very little value in a self-assessment that depends on the subject also supplying its evidence and changing the question when the result is unwelcome.

## Step 2 | Calculate the exercise score

Divide the points awarded by twice the number of applicable items, then multiply by 100. If there are no applicable items, record N/A and choose a new scenario. For example, 8 points from 5 applicable items gives 80%. All figures here are invented. No score denotes seniority, fitness to manage or leadership impact.

| Fictional band | Interpretation for discussion |
| --- | --- |
| Below 40% | Identify the smallest responsibility currently concealed by the process. |
| 40% to below 70% | The team probably knows some boundaries. Find the one that still depends on your presence. |
| 70% to below 90% | Examine the decision that will be hardest for someone else to reconstruct. |
| 90% and above | Ask whether the case was too easy. If the comparison is fair, decline to create a more complicated one for the sake of the dashboard. |

One group at the simulated Institute submitted 100% before selecting a case. I was imprudently impressed by how quickly they had completed the scoring. They had perfected a different technique, one involving the circulation of excellent figures free from the inconvenience of an observed initiative. I was able to congratulate them on finishing and ask them to start again in the same breath.

## Step 3 | Leave with one adjustment

Write one sentence the team should have heard earlier. Write the role that should have delivered it. If those two entries are clear, close the exercise. There is no official certification, although you may designate the paper an executive record and file it with satisfying precision.
