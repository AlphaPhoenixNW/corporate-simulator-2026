# Appendix D | A Thirty-Day Programme for Achieving Less

I describe these as days because the calendar industry understands the unit. You may take a shorter time, a longer time or a decisive break before day one. Use each prompt on optional work. Retain every real duty and meet it on its own terms. The programme does not grant credit for disappointing a person to whom you owe something.

## Days 1–7 | Observe the existing workload

| Day | Exercise | Finish when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Open one week's calendar and mark the items you remember accepting. Leave the others visible. | You can see which activities entered the week before you did. |
| 2 | Ask who would experience a relevant consequence if each of three commitments slipped. | You have three names or three clear absences. |
| 3 | List the last three tasks that returned to you after delegation. Write the authority or information they lacked. | You have described the boundary in language a new colleague could understand. |
| 4 | Take one recurring meeting. Ask what decision it made most recently and where someone can read it. | You find a record or have made an honest second entry for an absence of evidence. |
| 5 | Build activity, evidence, effect and obligation ledgers for yesterday. Read the obligation line first. | You can explain which work actually needed you. |
| 6 | Observe an interval in which you usually receive low-priority requests. Keep a count without intervening where there is no duty. | You know how many requests came with a real decision attached. |
| 7 | Give the week one sentence. Choose a modest sentence. Early maturity is measurable by the absence of a press statement. | You can close the page without creating day seven and a half. |

## Days 8–14 | Retire one unnecessary activity

| Day | Exercise | Finish when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Select an optional task with no promised effect. Ask its requester whether anything depends on it. | The requester has provided a reason or withdrawn it. |
| 9 | Apply STAY. Give the consequence of deferral more space than the advantages of finishing. | You can state the choice without substituting a synonym for progress. |
| 10 | If the work is unwanted, record a decision to retire it. Inform only the people who would wait. | Nobody is relying on silence to convey a change of plan. |
| 11 | Release the next calendar slot associated with the activity. Let its former occupants choose what happens there. | The slot has exited your remit. |
| 12 | Inspect any automatic reminders the activity left behind. Keep the ones that still serve real obligations. | The work cannot mysteriously return on a Thursday. |
| 13 | Perform one necessary task you previously hid among optional ones. Observe whether clarity affected its length. | The task is complete or its real blocker is recorded. |
| 14 | Write one sentence explaining what changed when the optional task disappeared. Record “no observed effect” if that is all you know. | You have resisted inventing a financial return. |

## Days 15–21 | Move an authority boundary into view

| Day | Exercise | Finish when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | Write a CLEAR card for a concrete task. Identify the action beside the authority required to take it. | Someone unfamiliar with the project can tell which belongs to them. |
| 16 | Ask the delegate to point to the first obstacle outside their control. | You know which role must decide, along with what the delegate can still complete. |
| 17 | Hold a small decision. Replace one status meeting with a question that has an owner. | The answer exists in a place the team can use. |
| 18 | Assign a returning item a narrower boundary. Give its owner the information they previously had to request. | They can proceed without a courtesy interrogation. |
| 19 | Review the last communications draft you wrote. Replace vague success with an identified actor, act and result. | The person who did the work could recognise themselves in the record. |
| 20 | Run a no-authority exercise. Imagine the last parameter is unavailable and review the proper stop. | Everyone can identify the permitted next step without inventing permission. |
| 21 | Read the decision record once while seated, reclined or horizontal. Add only the piece required to let a person act or stop. | The posture has ceased to affect the result. |

## Days 22–30 | Learn to stop at scale

| Day | Exercise | Finish when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 22 | Prepare a fictional dashboard row. Give its denominator an eligibility rule and identify an instance that would fail. | A person with the source data could reproduce the display. |
| 23 | Practise writing N/A with evident confidence. State exactly which eligible observation is missing. | The empty cell is neither green nor a reason to invent a customer. |
| 24 | Put one risk in a register. Give it a consequence and a control. Use a description a person could read without feeling accused. | Somebody can recognise when the risk matters. |
| 25 | Write a proposed board resolution that actually reaches a disposition. Leave the approval field empty during drafting. | A future reader can tell what was proposed and who could vote. |
| 26 | Move an ambition into an aspirational part of a plan. Remove its implied deadline unless a real commitment exists. | Nobody can mistake the entry for an instruction to proceed. |
| 27 | Protect one interval of your own time. Do not assign an enterprise purpose to the interval. | The interval has passed in whatever manner you chose. |
| 28 | Evaluate which activities in this programme have started to occupy more attention than they justify. Retire one. | The programme has demonstrated the ability to reduce its own scope. |
| 29 | Draft the final disposition. Retain one record of outcomes and describe the condition that could reopen a deferred item. | The record can be read without a scheduled tour. |
| 30 | Read the disposition. Return the workbook to a shelf or a sensible file. Allow the programme to terminate. | You have made no appointment for day thirty-one. |

If you reached day thirty early, I ask you to consider whether the retained time requires a special project. If the question makes you tired, you are under no obligation to answer it today. The Institute reserves a blank certificate for this precise achievement. In accordance with our distribution philosophy, it remains in the drawer.
