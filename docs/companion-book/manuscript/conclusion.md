# Conclusion | The Courage to Stay in Bed

When I turn the launch attempt over in my mind, I still admire how little of it was required to produce the precise dilemma in which I found myself. There had been substantial engineering. There had been real verification. There had been a careful decision to preserve private history and prepare a different object for the proposed destination. Once the routes we had tried met an access boundary, there remained a clear and ordinary step that somebody with the right access could take.

The smallness of the step mattered. A large demand would have carried a justification. Its size would have invited questions about scope, responsibility and timing. A small demand could enter the room unannounced and ask me to undertake it before I had thought about what it would claim afterwards. Large demands are accustomed to submitting documents. Small demands write themselves into the margins and often enjoy an uninterrupted life there.

I wanted a theory that could make this moment visible. I found that the useful part of delegating was being able to see precisely where the responsibility returned. The useful part of comfort was that it gave me a strong motive to examine an assumption I might otherwise have accepted. The useful part of governance was knowing which facts belonged on the record. I had to own the decision and its consequences. Once I did so, the decision no longer needed to consume the people who had done the work.

I also wanted to become a man whose decision to remain in bed could be described in a hardcover. This ambition has been amply served by the evidence.

I now address the leader who is reading this at the end of a very long day. If an obligation is yours and other people rely on it, make the arrangements it deserves. If there is a team waiting for a decision, give it one with a consequence they can understand. If a practical task belongs to another person, be clear enough in delegation that they can do it without managing your attention. And if the duty you have spent the evening trying to discover turns out to be a preference you are free to set aside, you may admit that there is a choice. You can write the history as it happened and leave the ambition on the page for a future owner decision.

Look around at the demands you were about to inherit. Some are substantial. Some merely stand near substantial demands so that you will mistake them for members of the same family. You can make those judgements from wherever you happen to be. I received unusually good information from the edge of a duvet. Other leaders may have access to a lookout or a small conservatory. The resource is still attention. Protect enough of it to see the difference.

The public did not receive the application that evening. The application could still do what we'd made it to do. The records tell you which happened, which failed and who decided to let the evening end. I read those records now with the pride of a founder who can finally answer the question of what he personally achieved.

I did not fail to launch. I succeeded in remaining where I was.
