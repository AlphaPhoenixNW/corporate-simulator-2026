# Chapter 10 | The Bed-Led Organisation™

> “I asked each department what it needed from me. The most mature departments had learnt to leave a gap after the question.” — The Founder of NOTHING™

A philosophy that depends entirely on the temperament of one man in a bed has limited prospects for scale. I therefore began to design an institution capable of extending the benefits of my absence to people I had never met. My first act was to imagine a finance department. Finance is useful in a fictional enterprise because it allows the founder to contemplate a budget without acquiring anybody's money. My second act was to give the department the right to ask questions about the first.

The **Bed-Led Organisation™** is a model company, separate from the small private project that gave me the idea. It is populated by fictional roles. Many have been kept deliberately vacant to protect cost assumptions. Its purpose is to allow engineering, operations, finance and management to collaborate without asking the founder to sit up each time two parts of the business discover one another.

At the top of the model I place a decision-making role rather than a bed. This is politically important. A bed that appears at the top of an organisational chart invites people to write their own version of the reporting lines. The founder holds the authority. The duvet holds a limited set of physical responsibilities. The finance department would like this separation made explicit when it receives the furniture request.

## The operating chart

| Reports to | Fictional executive role | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simulated board | Founder and Chief Executive | Decides scope, accepts residual risk and authorises publication. |
| Founder | Chief Operating Officer | Maintains the handoff from verified artefact to authorised operator. |
| Founder | Chief Engineering Officer | Delivers an inspectable artefact and keeps test evidence. |
| Founder | Chief Financial Officer | Records actual commitments and places imaginary returns in a clearly labelled column. |
| Founder | Director of Executive Comfort | Preserves quiet operating conditions and rejects requests for a second cushion unless one is needed. |
| Chief Operating Officer | Release Custodian | Keeps the approved artefact, checks the route and escalates access or authority questions. |
| Chief Financial Officer | Office of Unclaimed Efficiencies | Maintains a register of savings the company has insufficient evidence to claim. |

The simulated board sets terms for executive discretion. No role of the board votes in an actual release. This is important for the founder, who could otherwise discover that an imaginary board had issued an extremely real instruction to get up.

Engineering is insulated from pressure to invent business utility. Its responsibility is to build the thing it was asked to build and prove the observable contract. If somebody proposes a feature in the name of enterprise value, engineering asks for the consequence the feature will have. Where the proposed feature is a data export, the meeting may end early. There is very little benefit to drawing a detailed architecture for a document that will contain no fields.

Operations owns the point at which the artefact could move. Its interest is practical. Can an authorised terminal reach the destination? Does its identity have the required access? Are we moving the exact approved thing? Operations receives a decision if the answer requires the founder. It does not receive the founder as a seasonal replacement for access.

Finance has two ledgers. The first records actual transactions where they exist. The second contains return figures invented for training. I require simulated tables to look visibly different from the financial record. This prevents somebody sending a fictional saving to a real accountant. I was initially disappointed by the way this rule constrained the growth curve. After consideration I recognised the discipline as exactly the kind of enterprise maturity that would give the model the appearance of being fundable in the absence of funding.

## The committees

I keep only those committees essential to giving the model its proper scale. Memberships may overlap. Meetings may be cancelled on receipt of evidence that all members agree.

| Committee | Standing responsibility | Reason to convene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committee for the Elimination of Unnecessary Committees | Retire committees whose remits have been exhausted | A committee submits an agenda explaining why it still exists |
| Release Readiness and Restraint Committee | Review an approved route and a credible reason to use it | The owner asks to reopen release planning |
| Duvet Allocation Panel | Establish a sensible comfort boundary | An exceptional request proposes moving it |
| Evidence Retention Forum | Keep decisions findable without maintaining redundant dossiers | A record cannot be located by the person meant to use it |
| Finance Subcommittee on Hypothetical Savings | Keep estimates away from the accounts | A slide carries figures presented as actual savings |

Students sometimes propose a separate body to coordinate the cancellation of meetings across these five committees. I encourage them to submit the proposal directly to the first committee and experience the model operating at full capacity.

### A case from the Institute | The test of an absent chief

Our fictional company packages a zero-effect utility and sends a release handoff to the custodian. The custodian discovers a problem with access. The proposed launch is optional. The executive is absent. We ask students to run the model through three shifts, each represented by a single piece of paper.

Engineering shows the verified artefact. Operations records the blocked destination and its next permitted route. Finance checks whether a commitment was made that would change the decision; in the case it was not. The director of executive comfort receives no request, because the authorised report is sufficient. The founder sees a concise decision on reopening the release. There is no launch pending a real decision.

On the next run the board adds a real deadline and an authorised deputy. The custodian must get a decision to the deputy or escalate within the stated terms. The founder's comfort is still a valuable institutional interest, but it has ceased to be an argument for making other people pretend they have no responsibilities. This version is often the first exercise in which the chair of the model board discovers a reason to be useful. I have learnt to allow this progress.

## Representation without obstruction

The essential question of organisational design is what will happen when the role at the top disappears for an ordinary interval. If the answer is that every team goes quiet, the founder can regard this as evidence of a highly concentrated enterprise. I regard it as a sign that the leadership has located the same bottleneck in several departments and given each department a different name for it.

I prefer a structure in which the executive can remain physically inactive and people can still work on the things they have authority to finish. There will be moments when action depends on the founder. They should be visible and few. As the institution learns to bring forward precisely the question that requires attention, the founder can take pride in the quality of the brief interruptions that remain. I have occasionally experienced a good one under the duvet and been able to approve it without disturbing any of the corners.

### Reflection | Remove a reporting line

Draw the model for a small voluntary project. Identify one decision that belongs to the owner, one task that belongs to engineering and one check that belongs to operations. Add a finance role only if something can genuinely be committed or spent. Then remove every line leading to the person currently being copied in for reassurance. Count the decisions the chart can still make.
